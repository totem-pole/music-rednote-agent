# Music-first critical listening protocol

## Musical content must occupy a meaningful portion of review
Draft has 3 anchors minimum: (1) arrangement/instrumentation, (2) composition/structure/rhythm/harmony or lyrical form, (3) whole-album sequencing, sonic production or vocal technique. Each anchor names a specific track, concrete feature and interpretive relevance. If fewer than 3 supported anchors, do not publish as a review; label a background/profile essay instead.

## Per-track evidence matrix
| track | source of hearing | arrangement | groove/rhythm | melodic/harmonic shape | recording/mix/voice | lyrics | album-level role | uncertainty |
Do not force technical harmonic claims where no score/listening evidence exists. Distinguish "critic X describes fuzzy guitars" from "I heard fuzz". Do not invent sonic minutiae or timestamps. Best workflow: the editor listens to 3 tracks and supplies brief timestamps and notes.

## Distinguish influences and imitation
Genre or resemblance claims are hypotheses unless supported by audible comparison or attributed critic. E.g. calling something 'C86', 'shoegaze' or 'adult contemporary' should come with what textures, songwriting conventions or productions warrant that description.

## Thesis test
Could this text apply to 20 other albums? If yes, rewrite with actual musical specifics. Would removing artist biography leave no musical insight? If yes, reject.
